  <h1 class="fx__hero">Designing the firm of the future.</h1>
  <p class="fx__lead">How AI changes the way companies coordinate — and what to build so it changes them for the better.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <div class="fx__prose">
    <p>Every company runs on knowledge of its own work: who is doing what, what has already been decided, what happens next. Almost none of it is written down. It lives in whoever happens to hold it, and it decays every time someone joins, leaves, or gets busy.</p>

    <p>Organizations have spent a century compensating for that with structure — roles, hierarchy, process. My research is largely about what the compensation costs. Structure buys predictability, and predictability is worth a lot when work is routine. It is close to the wrong instrument when work has to change: <strong>the teams that adapt are the ones whose people can renegotiate coordination in real time</strong>, and rigid roles are exactly what stops them. Hand out autonomy without a shared view of the work and it does not get freer either — it quietly reorganizes around whoever already had context, with the predictable consequences for who ends up doing what.</p>

    <p>AI changes the terms of that trade. Companies now emit a continuous digital trace of their own coordination — messages, calendars, tickets, commits, decisions — and language models can finally read it at the scale it is produced. The model of the organization that used to live in people's heads can be built from what the company already does, kept current, and acted on.</p>

    <p><strong>That is the interesting problem.</strong> Not flatter orgs or faster meetings — a firm that actually knows what it is doing, and that humans and agents can coordinate through instead of around.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2 class="fx__title">Three problems I keep coming back to</h2>
  <ol class="fx__steps">
    <li>
      <div>
        <div class="fx__step-head">A live model of the work</div>
        <p class="fx__step-sub">What would it take for a company to hold an accurate, current picture of its own tasks, skills, and decisions — assembled from the traces it already produces, rather than from another form somebody has to fill in?</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li>
      <div>
        <div class="fx__step-head">Human–agent coordination</div>
        <p class="fx__step-sub">Agents are becoming coworkers. When does putting one inside a team measurably improve the team's decisions, and when is it just another actor with no idea what the rest of the company is doing?</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li>
      <div>
        <div class="fx__step-head">Coordination you can actually measure</div>
        <p class="fx__step-sub">Most claims about organization design are untestable in practice. Digital traces plus causal inference make them testable — at the scale of millions of interactions, in the field rather than the lab.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
  </ol>
